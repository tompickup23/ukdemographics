**53% White British in 2011. 43% in 2021. The trajectory does not stop.**

Census 2021. Birmingham. 42.9% White British. Down from 53.1% ten years earlier. Ten percentage points. One decade. Of the English local authorities with more than 500,000 residents, only Manchester fell faster, by 10.6 points.

The numbers going forward:

| Year | White British |
|------|-------------|
| 2011 | 53.1% |
| 2021 | 42.9% |
| 2031 | 32.8% |
| 2041 | 22.9% |
| 2051 | 14.9% |
| 2061 | 9.1% |

Four things drive this.

**Fertility.** White British total fertility rate nationally: 1.31 children per woman, computed from ONS 2024 births by ethnicity against Census 2021 women aged 15 to 44. Pakistani: 2.52. Birmingham's Pakistani population is 17.0%. That fertility gap compounds every year. Every cohort is less White British than the last.

**Age structure.** Birmingham's White British population skews older. The younger cohorts are already majority non-White-British. White British pupils are 24.7% of the city's school roll in 2024/25, against 42.9% of the population. The demographic wave is already in the system.

**White flight.** Birmingham gained 71,877 residents between the two Censuses and lost 79,006 White British ones, from 570,217 to 491,211. Every other broad group grew. Census 2021 origin-destination data is not in this site's dataset, so this piece names no destination.

**International settlement.** Birmingham is a major settlement city. 1.2% of the population had moved to it from outside the UK in the year before Census 2021. South Asian and African countries dominate the recent flow: Pakistan and India together account for 42.3% of National Insurance registrations to overseas nationals in Birmingham, with Nigeria, Bangladesh, China and Eritrea behind them.

**The models agree on direction.** The Hamilton-Perry model projects 14.9% White British by 2051, revised up from 10.7% when the model was recalibrated on 13 August 2026: the settings it used before were projecting change too fast. The cohort-component model, which assumes ethnic fertility rates converge partway to the national mean, projects 21.7% on the same year. The true outcome sits between them. Both agree Birmingham passed majority non-White-British years ago; the question is how fast it continues.

**Religion.** Birmingham's Muslim population: 31.8% in 2021. Christian: 36.2%. Both are Census 2021 TS030 shares of the residents who answered the religion question, which is the scaling the place page uses. The projection puts Muslim at 47.5% in 2041 and 53.5% in 2051, so the crossing falls in the middle of the 2040s. First major English city.

Birmingham is not the exception. It is the preview. Leicester, Luton, Slough: similar trajectories. Blackburn with Darwen: projected minority White British by 2027. The dynamics are national. Birmingham just gets there first.
