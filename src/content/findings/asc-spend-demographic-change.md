**What the published figures show.** Adult social care is run by upper tier
councils: counties, unitary authorities, metropolitan districts and London
boroughs. A district council has no adult social care budget, so a district has
no spend figure of its own.

Of the five councils this piece named as changing fastest, Blackburn with Darwen
spent **£742** per adult in 2025 to 2026 and West Northamptonshire **£553**. Pendle,
Preston and Burnley are Lancashire districts. Of the five named as changing
slowest, Wyre, Ribble Valley, Fylde, West Lancashire and Rossendale are all
Lancashire districts. Lancashire County Council spent **£729** per adult. Blackpool
spent **£926**, and England as a whole **£675**.

Source: Department of Health and Social Care, Adult social care finance report, England: 2025 to 2026, Table 14, divided by ONS mid 2025 population estimates for residents aged 18 and over. Accessed 2 November 2026.

These figures count gross current expenditure on adult social care in the
financial year, divided by residents aged 18 and over. They are spend per adult
resident, not per person receiving care.

**Methodological limitation:** A comparison of a handful of councils is
descriptive, not causal. Spend per adult follows deprivation, the share of
residents aged 65 and over, unpaid care and local policy, so differences between
councils are not a measure of need or efficiency on their own.

Whether immigration driven demographic change increases or decreases future ASC demand depends on the age profile of incoming populations. Younger migrant populations may temporarily reduce the 65+ share, but eventually age into the care system themselves.
