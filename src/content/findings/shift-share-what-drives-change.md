**Burnley's ethnic change is not mainly about Burnley.** It's about Britain.

The 2011 to 2021 White British decline in every local authority has been decomposed into three components: national trend, age structure, and local migration. In Burnley, the breakdown is:

- **National effect: -6.1pp** (79% of total change)
- **Age structure: -0.4pp** (5%)
- **Local migration: -1.2pp** (16%)

Burnley's total WBI decline: -7.6pp (from 85.5% to 77.9%). 79% of that is the national trend. Burnley would have seen most of the same change even with zero local migration, because the White British share fell from 80.5% to 74.4% across England and Wales as a whole.

The national trend is the largest of the three components in 276 of the 318 areas. The local component is the largest in the other 42. In 27 of those it pulls the White British share down faster than the national trend, among them Barking and Dagenham, Birmingham, Hillingdon, Luton, Manchester, Slough, Watford and Cambridge. In the other 15, mostly rural areas such as the Isle of Anglesey, North Norfolk and Torridge, it works the other way: the White British share fell by less than the national trend.

**Data**: ONS Census 2011 table KS201EW (ethnic group) and Census 2021 table TS021 (ethnic group), local authorities in England and Wales, via NOMIS. The national effect is calculated over all 318 authorities; for the eleven created after 2011, the 2011 figure is the sum of the 2011 districts that formed them. Percentage shares of Burnley's change, and the counts of which component is largest, are taken from the unrounded components, so in a few areas where two components round to the same figure the place page shows them as equal. Shift-share methodology following Franklin (2014, *Population, Space and Place*).
