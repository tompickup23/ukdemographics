**Burnley's White British share fell from 85.5% to 77.9% between 2011 and 2021.** A descriptive calculation separates that change into:

- **National trend: -6.1 percentage points**, or 80% of Burnley's change.
- **Composition adjustment: -0.4 percentage points**, or 5%.
- **Local residual: -1.2 percentage points**, or 15%.

Burnley's total change is -7.6 percentage points. Components and the total are rounded separately, so the displayed components do not add exactly to the displayed total.

The national component is the change in White British share across the summed counts of all 318 local authorities. The composition adjustment multiplies the difference between an area's starting share and the national starting share by the national proportional change. The residual is the local change left after subtracting those two components.

This calculation describes how local change compares with an aggregate benchmark. It uses no age or migration observations, so it cannot say how much of the change was caused by either, or what would have happened without migration.

The national component is the largest in absolute size in 275 of the 318 areas. The residual is largest in the other 43. It is negative in 28 of those and positive in 15. A positive residual offsets part of the decline represented by the other components. The place pages name the largest component in the direction of the overall change and identify a larger opposing component where present.

**Data and method:** ONS Census 2011 table KS201EW and Census 2021 table TS021, local authorities in England and Wales, via Nomis bulk downloads. For the eleven councils created after 2011, the 2011 count is the exact sum of the districts that formed them. The national component uses the sums of those local authority rows. These give 59,597,569 residents and 44,355,040 White British residents in 2021; the published England and Wales row gives 59,597,542 and 44,355,038 respectively. Census disclosure controls mean rows at different geographic levels need not add exactly. Both yield a White British share of 74.4% when rounded to one decimal place.

Percentages of Burnley's change and counts of the largest component use unrounded components. Place chart labels use the displayed one-decimal components, so ties at that precision are named equally. This is the site's descriptive share calculation, not an age-cohort decomposition.
