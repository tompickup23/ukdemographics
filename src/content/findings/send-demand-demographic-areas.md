**Pupils with an EHC plan in England rose by 65.4% in five years**, from 325,618
in 2020/21 to 538,547 in 2025/26. In 2025/26 they are 6.0% of pupils.

The areas this piece first named:

- **Blackpool**: +70.0%, from 843 to 1,433 pupils
- **Lancashire**, which runs SEND for Preston: +75.2%, from 6,720 to 11,771
- **Doncaster**: +59.4%, from 1,432 to 2,282
- **North Northamptonshire**: no five-year figure, because the council was created in April 2021

Growth is fast almost everywhere, and it does not track demographic change.
Across the 123 unitary, metropolitan and London authorities with both figures,
the rank correlation between five-year growth and the 2011 to 2021 change in the
White British share is -0.04, which is no relationship at all.

Rising EHC plan numbers reflect multiple factors: improved identification practices, changing diagnostic thresholds, increased parental awareness, and genuine prevalence changes. Language barriers in areas with higher non-English-speaking populations may also affect identification timing.

**What the figures count.** Pupils on the January school census who have an EHC
plan, in all schools. Plans held by children under school age, young people in
further education and those not in school are not included. SEND is run by
upper-tier councils, so a district has no figure of its own.

**Methodological limitation:** This is a descriptive comparison without statistical controls. EHC plan growth correlates with deprivation, population growth and council assessment practice, independently of demographic change.

The question for local authorities is whether SEND funding models keep pace with demand that has grown by two thirds in five years.
